Growth investing guide

A simple way to start growth investing

Choose an amount you can afford, automate it into a broad low-cost fund, reinvest dividends, and give it time.

What broad-market index funds are

Funds such as those that track the S&P 500 (for example VOO or similar) give you ownership in hundreds of large U.S. companies through a single investment. They are designed for long-term total return, not for high current income.

Important: Stock market investments can and do decline in value, sometimes for extended periods. There is no guarantee of positive returns.

What makes growth investing work over long periods

1

Regular contributions

Adding money consistently (dollar-cost averaging) reduces the impact of trying to time the market.

2

Time in the market

Historically, longer holding periods have increased the probability of positive real returns for broad equity indexes, though past results do not guarantee future ones.

3

Low costs + reinvestment

Lower expense ratios leave more of the return in your account. Reinvesting dividends compounds growth.

The simple takeaway

Choose a realistic amount & cadence

Start with money and a schedule you can maintain for years.

Keep costs low

Prefer low expense ratios when possible.

Give it time

Stay focused on the long-term plan rather than short-term price moves.

DRIP (Dividend Reinvestment)
On = dividends buy more shares of the same positions. Off = dividends are paid as cash.
How to fill positions correctly:
Enter the current number of shares you own, the current price per share, and the current annual dividend the position pays per share.

• The model treats “Annual dividend per share” as fixed over time (a standard beginner-friendly assumption).
• As new money is added or DRIP reinvests dividends, the share count increases → total income rises automatically.
• You do not need to change the dividend-per-share number just because you own more shares.
• Payouts per year tells the model how often the company pays (4 = quarterly is most common).
• DRIP is controlled only by the global toggle above (On or Off). Fractional shares are assumed when DRIP is On.

Dividend investing guide

A simple Core + Portfolio system

Keep cash earning interest in Core. Fund Core regularly so it never runs dry. Move money from Core into the portfolio on a schedule you can stick with. Decide whether to reinvest dividends or take them as cash.

What a dividend is

A dividend is a portion of a company’s profits paid to shareholders. Yield is the annual dividend divided by the current price. Dividends can be reduced or eliminated; they are not guaranteed.

Core vs Portfolio

Core (your vessel)

Cash or money-market holding (for example SPAXX or a high-yield savings account). You add money to it on one cadence and send money out of it on another. It is completely separate from the investment portfolio.

Portfolio

The actual dividend-paying investments. This is where long-term income is built.

Price growth vs pure income focus

If your primary goal is current income, setting expected price growth to 0% is a reasonable and conservative planning assumption. It means the illustration does not rely on share prices rising. In reality many dividend-paying investments have historically shown some long-term price appreciation, but that is not guaranteed and should not be required for an income plan to work.

If you are still in the accumulation phase and also expect some capital growth, you may enter a modest positive number. Always treat any growth assumption as uncertain.

DRIP explained

DRIP on

Dividends automatically buy more shares of the same positions. This is typically useful while you are still building the portfolio.

DRIP off

Dividends arrive as cash you can spend or redeploy. This is useful when you need the income.

The simple takeaway

Fund Core regularly

So it never runs out of money to send to the portfolio.

Transfer on a schedule

Consistent moves from Core → Portfolio beat trying to time the market.

Decide on DRIP

Reinvest while building; take cash when you need the income.
