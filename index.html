Growth investing guide

A simple way to start growth investing

Choose an amount you can afford, automate it into a broad low-cost fund, reinvest dividends, and give it time.

What VOO is

VOO is an ETF designed to track the S&P 500. Buying it means you own tiny pieces of hundreds of large U.S. companies through one simple investment.

Important: VOO can (and will) go down in value.

What makes growth investing work

1

Regular contributions

Add money consistently on a cadence you can stick with.

2

Time

The longer money stays invested, the more opportunity it has to potentially grow.

3

Low costs + reinvestment

Low expense ratios and reinvested dividends help more of the return stay in your account.

Why fees matter

Even small annual fees compound against you. Prefer low-cost index funds so more of the market’s return stays yours.

The simple takeaway

Choose a realistic amount & cadence

Start with money and a schedule you can maintain.

Keep costs low

Prefer low expense ratios.

Give it time

Dividend investing guide

A simple way to use a Core + Portfolio system

Keep cash earning interest in a Core account. Move money from Core into a dividend portfolio on a schedule you can stick with. Decide whether to reinvest the dividends or take them as income.

What a dividend is

A dividend is a portion of a company’s profits paid to shareholders. Yield is the annual dividend divided by price. A 3.5% yield on a $50,000 portfolio would generate about $1,750 per year if the dividend stays the same.

Core vs Portfolio

Core (your vessel)

Cash or money-market holding (SPAXX, HYSA, etc.). Earns interest. Acts as storage and the source of new investments. Completely separate from the portfolio.

Portfolio

The actual dividend-paying investments. This is where you want long-term growth of income.

DRIP explained

DRIP on

Dividends automatically buy more shares. Best while you are still building.

DRIP off

Dividends arrive as cash you can spend. Useful when you need income.

Simple setup

  1. Decide how much sits in Core vs already invested in the portfolio.
  2. Choose a transfer amount and cadence from Core → Portfolio that you can maintain.
  3. Pick a realistic overall yield assumption (many broad dividend ETFs sit roughly in the 2.5–4% range).
  4. Turn DRIP on while building; turn it off when you want the cash.
  5. Review once or twice a year.

The simple takeaway

Core is storage + interest

Keep it separate. Use it to fund the portfolio.

Move money on a schedule

Consistent transfers beat perfect timing.

Decide on DRIP

Reinvest while building; take cash when you need income.
